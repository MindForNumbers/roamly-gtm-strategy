## 1. Pricing goal

_Use your M2 strategic bet and competitive intelligence. Is Roamly entering a new market, maximizing revenue from a known audience, or defending a position?_

- **Goal:** Maximize revenue from a known audience
- **Why this is the right commercial goal now:** Our existing users are already actively pulling for this solution, evidenced by high multi-ticket cart abandonment and support tickets. Winning right now means stopping this exact checkout bleeding. Capturing value from this known audience is the right commercial goal because the demand is proven—making this a low-lift brand evolution rather than a cold, expensive market entry. Furthermore, this directly ties back to our M2 strategic bet (our PLG motion): by seamlessly unblocking our known Organizers, we instantly unlock stranded transaction GMV while simultaneously fueling our viral acquisition loop as they invite their friends to pay their share.

## 2. Pricing model

_The right model reflects how the customer experiences value. Watch the default to subscription._

- **Model:** Transaction-based
- **Who pays — and for what?** _Does the buyer experience value differently from the end user?_

The payment structure is flexible: every individual pays their exact fractional share via the split-payment link, or one person can opt to cover the entire booking upfront. The Organizer (the power user) experiences value through administrative relief and the removal of personal financial risk. The Invitees (the end users) experience value as a frictionless checkout to settle their debt and instantly access the trip plans. Because this value is realized at the exact moment a milestone trip is successfully funded, we capture it via a per-transaction service fee at checkout.

## 3. Packaging approach

_How many distinct audiences (from M4)? Does one structure serve them all, or do different groups need different options?_

- **Structure:** By features
- **Audiences & fit:** We identified three distinct audiences in M4: the Organizer, the Invitee, and the Host. A single overarching "By Features" structure serves them all dynamically. The Organizer needs to see the full administrative suite (split-payment generation, itinerary hub) to adopt the tool, while the Invitee only needs to see their fractional price and a frictionless checkout button to convert.

| Tier | Who it's for | Price | What's included |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Standard Checkout** | Solo & couple travelers (1–3 tickets) | Standard booking fee | Single-card checkout, baseline host reservation, self-managed trip details. |
| **Roamly Groups ★** | Milestone Organizers & Invitees (4+ tickets) | Free feature access (standard transaction take-rate) | Native split-payment links, individual payment tracking, automated reminders, and the centralized shared itinerary hub. |
| **Roamly Concierge** | High-budget groups & corporate offsites | Flat concierge retainer + standard transaction take-rate | Full Roamly Groups toolset plus a dedicated local host/expert who custom-curates and coordinates multi-day group itineraries. |

## 4. Research to validate

_A PM doesn't run these — name what you'd commission and the specific assumption you're testing. Recruit against your ICP._

- **Method:** Van Westendorp
- **Assumption to validate:** We are validating the exact acceptable transaction service fee range (the take-rate percentage) that Invitees and Organizers will tolerate on a split checkout before considering it "too expensive," ensuring the fee does not induce checkout abandonment.
- **The one thing that would change everything if you got it wrong:** The Invitee’s price sensitivity to the transaction fee. If the convenience fee is priced too high, Invitees will balk and revert to manual Venmo transfers; if even one Invitee abandons the link, the cart fails to reach 100% funding, the booking collapses, and the viral PLG acquisition loop breaks.

## 5. The one-page recommendation

> We recommend a **transaction-based model packaged by features (ungated V1)** for Roamly Groups, to **maximize revenue and recapture stranded GMV from a known audience**, because **multi-ticket cart abandonment is actively driven by single-payer checkout friction, and unblocking the organizer turns every invited friend into an authenticated user at zero CAC.**

For each stakeholder, what they most need to hear:

- **Finance** (margin & revenue model): Keeping the software free in V1 avoids SaaS churn risks and operational overhead, immediately unlocking high-basket transaction GMV from the 4+ ticket carts currently abandoning checkout.
- **Sales Leadership / Host Operations** (it closes deals): Verified group size tagging and guaranteed split-payment hold mechanics protect host inventory from flaking while driving higher volume and earnings per available time slot.
- **Product Marketing** (the story that lands): "Lock in your group trip without floating the cash or playing debt collector"—a pure PLG story where split checkout and shared itineraries turn group organizers into advocates and invitees into new users.

**Validate first:** Field a Van Westendorp Price Sensitivity study with churned multi-ticket cart abandoners to determine the exact take-rate threshold before invitees perceive the convenience fee as prohibitive and defect to Venmo.

### What I changed after AI review

- **What I changed:** Reframed the free V1 feature tier from a "giveaway" into a deliberate high-margin GMV recovery mechanism; elevated the shared itinerary workspace alongside split payments across all packaging tiers; mapped the fatal chain-reaction risk showing how high fees lead to invitee drop-out and 100% funding collapse; and tailored the defense specifically across Finance, Host Operations, and Product Marketing stakeholders.
- **Why:** The raw output defaulted to generic software feature lists. These adjustments translate platform mechanics into executive commercial arguments that justify ungating the software, preserve the viral PLG acquisition loop, and prove why empirical research is required before finalizing checkout take-rates.
